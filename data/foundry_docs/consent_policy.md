# Informed Consent Requirements

**Synthetic data. No PHI. Not clinical decision support.**

Informed consent is an ongoing process, not a single signature event. Before enrollment in any AMC IQ clinical trial, the research team must provide the prospective participant with the IRB-approved consent form, adequate time for questions, and a discussion appropriate to the person's clinical context, language needs, and decision-making preferences.

Consent must be obtained before any study-specific procedure that is not part of routine clinical care. Review of existing records for feasibility may occur under approved preparatory-to-research workflows, but protocol-mandated tests, questionnaires, investigational treatment, or research-only sample collection may not begin until consent is documented.

The consent discussion should describe the purpose of the study, why the person is being asked to consider participation, expected visits and procedures, foreseeable risks, possible benefits, alternatives, privacy protections, costs or reimbursements when applicable, and voluntary participation. Participants must be told that declining a trial will not reduce access to standard oncology care at **AMC Thoracic Oncology Clinic (SITE-01)**.

The person obtaining consent must be listed on the delegation log and trained for the study. For thoracic oncology trials, coordinators such as **COORD-01, Dana Whitfield**, may support the process when delegated, but clinical questions about disease status, alternatives, or uncertain eligibility should be answered by the investigator or treating oncologist.

Signed consent forms must be stored in the approved research record and a copy provided to the participant. If new information may affect willingness to continue, re-consent or an IRB-approved notification process is required.
