# EGFR-Mutant Metastatic NSCLC Pathway Summary

**Synthetic data. No PHI. Not clinical decision support.**

This internal pathway summarizes the AMC IQ approach to metastatic NSCLC with EGFR alterations, including EGFR exon 20 insertion, when trial enrollment is being considered. It is written for care coordination and documentation consistency and does not replace clinician judgment or protocol-specific criteria.

At diagnosis or progression, the thoracic oncology team should confirm histology, stage, ECOG performance status, disease tempo, prior systemic therapy, and molecular findings from a validated assay. For uncommon EGFR variants such as exon 20 insertion, the record should distinguish the alteration from common sensitizing EGFR variants because treatment options and trial availability may differ.

When an appropriate trial is open at **AMC Thoracic Oncology Clinic (SITE-01)**, the treating oncologist should consider referral for screening before selecting the next systemic therapy, especially when the trial addresses the documented driver alteration. The referral should include recent labs, prior regimen details, response history, imaging status, and the molecular report.

If a patient is not eligible for a trial, the standard-of-care discussion should focus on approved systemic options appropriate to the alteration, prior treatment exposure, organ function, patient preferences, and expected toxicity profile. Supportive care needs, symptom control, and clinical monitoring should be addressed in parallel. For patients with renal impairment or other comorbidities, dose selection and regimen feasibility should be reviewed carefully.

Patients who narrowly miss a trial criterion may be reconsidered if the protocol allows re-screening and updated clinical information resolves the issue. However, pathway review should not delay medically necessary treatment when trial eligibility is unlikely or unresolved after PI review.
